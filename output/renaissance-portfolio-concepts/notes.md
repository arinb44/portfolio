## Milestone 1: Renaissance visual vocabulary and image rights (RQ1, RQ2)

### Techniques and formats

**[S1] Closer to Van Eyck: Rediscovering the Ghent Altarpiece (coverage)**
- Publisher: CODART; Getty Foundation news; site by KIK-IRPA, coordinated by Ron Spronk
- Date: site updates reported 2010s–2020; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://www.codart.nl/other/ghent-altarpiece-website-enhanced/ ; https://www.getty.edu/news/more-to-explore-as-the-ghent-altarpiece-website-gets-an-update
- Summary: Web viewer for the Ghent Altarpiece (van Eyck, 1432) with zoomable high-resolution photos, macrophotography, infrared reflectography (IRR, which shows the underdrawing beneath the paint) and X-radiography. You can compare the layers side by side. IRR shows changes of plan, e.g. two trees added after the sky was painted.
- Reliability: high (museum network and funder describing a scholarly project). Exact site URL not confirmed in search results; verify before citing in the report.
- Design use: "look beneath the paint" is a real research interaction. It is a precedent for revealing project process (sketch → build) by peeling layers.

**[S2] Masaccio, Holy Trinity; Early applications of linear perspective**
- Publisher: Smarthistory (Zucker & Harris); Britannica, "Linear perspective"
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://smarthistory.org/early-applications-of-linear-perspective ; https://www.britannica.com/art/linear-perspective
- Summary: Brunelleschi demonstrated linear perspective c. 1415; Alberti wrote it down in *De pictura*/*Della pittura* (1435). Masaccio's Holy Trinity (c. 1425–28, Santa Maria Novella) is a very early painted use. Its single vanishing point is near the viewer's eye level, and orthogonals converge to it. Dates for the fresco differ slightly between sources.
- Reliability: high (Smarthistory is peer-reviewed art-history education; Britannica is an encyclopedia). The "first" claims are repeated attributions, not settled fact.
- Design use: a vanishing-point grid can be a navigation system; the camera "travels" along orthogonals into a project.

**[S3] Eclectic Light Co., "Hard reality: 9 Linear perspective projection"**
- Author: Howard Oakley
- Date: 2023-06-15
- URL: https://eclecticlight.co/2023/06/15/hard-reality-9-linear-perspective-projection/
- Summary: Reports that Masaccio set a nail at the vanishing point and used string to construct the orthogonals.
- Reliability: medium (well-informed blog, secondary). Use as colour, not as a key claim.
- Design use: construction lines (nail and string) as an animated "drawing-in" of each page's layout.

**[S4] Sfumato and Leonardo's glazes (Walter et al., 2010, Louvre/C2RMF/ESRF)**
- Publisher: ESRF news; EurekAlert; Nature news (doi:10.1038/466694a)
- Date: July 2010
- URL: https://esrf.fr/news/general-old/general-2010/new-light-on-leonardo-da-vinci2019s-faces ; https://doi.org/10.1038/466694a
- Summary: X-ray fluorescence on seven Louvre paintings, including the Mona Lisa, found glaze layers about 1–2 µm thick, with total build-up no more than 30–40 µm. Sfumato blurs outlines and contours by stacking very thin translucent layers. The fingertip-application idea comes from a single commentary.
- Reliability: high for the measurements (peer-reviewed study reported by the research facility); low for the fingertip claim.
- Design use: sfumato as a motion principle. Elements never pop in; they condense out of haze through many thin layers (blur + opacity steps).

**[S5] "Painting in Oil in the Low Countries and Its Spread to Southern Europe"**
- Author: Susan Jones, The Met Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History
- Date: October 2002
- URL: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/painting-in-oil-in-the-low-countries-and-its-spread-to-southern-europe
- Summary: Oil makes most pigments translucent, so colour can be built up in thin glazes, giving the glowing reds and greens of e.g. the Arnolfini Portrait. Van Eyck did not invent oil painting (it is recorded from the 12th century). Early Netherlandish painters' mastery of the medium on panel was the turning point. Slow drying allowed soft, seamless blending.
- Reliability: high (museum scholarly essay).
- Design use: Northern "glaze" layering as a colour and lighting system. Deep, saturated jewel tones built from transparent layers over a light ground.

**[S6] Altarpiece formats: predella, polyptych, wings, grisaille exteriors**
- Publisher: The National Gallery, London (glossary "polyptych"); The Courtauld (Fra Angelico predella panels); Philadelphia Museum of Art (grisaille Annunciation); Wikipedia "Altarpiece" / "Winged altar"
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/glossary/polyptych ; https://gallerycollections.courtauld.ac.uk/object-p-1966-gp-10 ; https://www.philamuseum.org/objects/102122 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altarpiece
- Summary: The predella is the base row of small, often narrative, panels below the main images. A polyptych has many panels, and its hinged side panels are "wings". Wings were painted on both sides, so open and closed states show completely different imagery. Closed exteriors, especially Netherlandish ones of the 15th–16th c., were often in grisaille (grey monochrome imitating stone sculpture). Many altarpieces were later dismantled and scattered across museums.
- Reliability: high (museum sources); Wikipedia used only for corroboration.
- Design use: very strong structural metaphor for a portfolio. Main panel = featured project; predella = row of small project scenes; closed (grisaille) vs open (full colour) = summary vs detail state; scattered panels "reassembled" = projects connected into one whole.

**[S7] Holbein, The Ambassadors (1533), anamorphic skull**
- Publisher: The National Gallery, London (NG1314); Smarthistory; Wikipedia
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/NG1314 ; https://smarthistory.org/hans-holbein-the-younger-the-ambassadors
- Summary: The stretched shape between the men's feet reads as a skull only from a particular angle. Wikipedia: high on the right or low on the left. The 1996–97 restoration recalculated parts of the skull.
- Reliability: high (NG page). An unofficial site (internationaltrucks.com) claiming an NG simulator was discarded as unverified.
- Design use: anamorphosis as a scroll/pointer reveal. A distorted shape resolves into a project title or image when the user reaches the "right angle" (scroll position).

**[S8] Raphael drawings and cartoons; spolvero (pouncing)**
- Publisher: The National Gallery, London, "Studying Raphael: drawings and cartoons"; Smarthistory, Italian Renaissance drawing
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/research/studying-raphael-drawings-and-cartoons ; https://smarthistory.org/?p=58627
- Summary: The cartoon is a full-scale drawing used to transfer a design. In pouncing (spolvero, from "dust"), the outlines are pricked and charcoal or pigment dust is tapped through the holes, leaving dotted lines that are then joined up. Infrared shows pounce dots beneath Raphael's paint.
- Reliability: high (National Gallery research resource).
- Design use: page transitions as "transfer". A dotted outline appears first, then is joined up into line, then painted. Good loading-state metaphor.

**[S9] Mantegna, Camera degli Sposi ceiling oculus (completed 1474, Mantua)**
- Publisher: Wikipedia; DailyArt Magazine
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_degli_Sposi ; https://www.dailyartmagazine.com/andrea-mantegnas-bridal-chamber/
- Summary: A painted fake oculus opens to sky, with foreshortened putti leaning over a balustrade, seen *di sotto in sù* (from below). Often called the first or one of the first such ceilings; it influenced Correggio and Baroque ceilings.
- Reliability: medium (secondary sources agree; "first" is disputed).
- Design use: looking "up" through an opening into another space, e.g. a hero scene where scrolling tilts the camera up into the sky.

**[S10] Craquelure**
- Publisher: Wikipedia "Craquelure" (summarizing Spike Bucklow's classification); Bratasz et al. 2020, *Heritage Science*
- Date: n.d.; 2020
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craquelure ; https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s40494-020-0352-0.pdf
- Summary: Italian panel paintings (1300–1500) tend to show cracks perpendicular to the wood grain, jagged, with secondary networks. Flemish panels (1400–1600) show cracks parallel to the grain, smoother and straighter, with small square "islands". Cracks come from drying, shock, ageing and the layer structure. Crack patterns alone are unreliable for attribution.
- Reliability: medium (classification via Wikipedia summary of Bucklow; primary papers not read). Good enough for texture design, not for factual claims.
- Design use: procedural craquelure texture (e.g. Voronoi-based shader) that differs between "Italian" and "Flemish" themed sections.

**[S11] Gold ground and punchwork (tempera on panel)**
- Publisher: Smarthistory, "Gold-ground panel painting" and "Remaking a fourteenth-century triptych"; The Met essay "Italian Painting of the Later Middle Ages"
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://smarthistory.org/making-triptych ; https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/italian-painting-of-the-later-middle-ages
- Summary: Process: gesso ground, then gold leaf, burnished, then punched and incised decoration, then egg-tempera painting. Gold and costly pigments were kept for the most important panels.
- Reliability: medium-high (Smarthistory reconstruction video/essay). The Heilbrunn punchwork essay itself was not found.
- Design use: burnished gold that catches a moving light (pointer = candle). Punched halo patterns as hover states.

**[S15] EU Directive 2019/790, Article 14**
- Publisher: legislation.gov.uk (text); Kluwer Copyright Blog commentary

**[S16] Italy, Cultural Heritage Code Arts. 107–108 and Ministerial Decree 161/2023**
- Publisher: Communia Association; Kluwer Copyright Blog; Europeana
- Date: 2022–2023
- URL: https://communia-association.org/2023/07/10/tales-of-public-domain-protection-in-italy/ ; https://copyrightblog.kluweriplaw.com/2023/06/15/michelangelos-david-and-cultural-heritage-images-the-italian-pseudo-intellectual-property-and-the-end-of-public-domain/
- Summary: Italian state institutions (e.g. the Uffizi) can require authorization and fees for reproductions of works they hold, even public-domain ones. The Uffizi has sued over uses of Botticelli's Birth of Venus. Decree 161/2023 sets minimum fees. Non-commercial uses were partly liberalized. Exact current rules were not confirmed from the official text.
- Reliability: medium (advocacy and legal commentary; consistent with each other).
- **Implication:** source painting images from CC0 collections (Met, NGA, and others to check such as Rijksmuseum, Art Institute of Chicago, Cleveland). Treat images of works held in Italian state museums as restricted unless their terms are checked. A personal portfolio used to get jobs or clients may count as commercial.

### Milestone 1 takeaways
- The richest structural metaphors are the altarpiece (main panel + predella + wings that open), the layered painting (underdrawing → glazes → varnish → cracks), and perspective construction (vanishing point and orthogonals).
- The richest motion principles: sfumato (emerge from haze), spolvero (dotted transfer → line → paint), anamorphosis (resolve at the right angle), glazing (build colour in thin layers), the oculus (look up into another space).
- Image sourcing: favour CC0 museums. Italian state-held works carry legal risk.

## Milestone 2: Rendering and animation techniques (RQ3, RQ4)

**[S17] "On Crafting Painterly Shaders"**
- Author: Maxime Heckel (blog)
- Date: 2024 (exact date not confirmed); accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/on-crafting-painterly-shaders
- Summary: Builds an oil-paint look in real time as a post-processing pass with the Kuwahara filter. The filter picks, from several neighbouring regions, the one with the lowest colour variance and outputs its mean, so it smooths like a brush while keeping edges and corners. Anisotropic/directional variants follow edge flow for a more painterly result.
- Reliability: high for technique (well-known creative developer, code-backed); the Kuwahara algorithm is corroborated by Kodeco and Blender docs.
- Design use: any live 3D or video content (including project screenshots and demos) can be rendered "as oil paint" consistently. Strength can animate from photo → painting.

**[S27] MDN: `<feTurbulence>` (with `<feDisplacementMap>`)**
- Publisher: MDN Web Docs
- Date: n.d.; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Element/feTurbulence
- Summary: Generates Perlin-noise textures (clouds, marble). Feeding it into `feDisplacementMap` warps edges organically. Baseline "Widely available" since July 2015.

**[S28] Depth-map "fake 3D" and relighting (Codrops)**
- Author/Publisher: Yuriy Artyukh (2019); Dominik Fojcik (Relighting, n.d.); Codrops
- Date: 2019; 2025–2026
- URL: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/02/20/how-to-create-a-fake-3d-image-effect-with-webgl/ ; https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=119000
- Summary: A depth map (greyscale, white = near) lets a shader shift near pixels more than far ones with pointer movement, which gives a 2.5D parallax on a flat image. Depth maps can be estimated with models (MiDaS, Depth Anything). An 8-bit depth needs blurring to avoid stepping. Relighting uses depth/normals to move a light across an image.
- Reliability: high for technique (working tutorials).
- Design use: turn CC0 painting images into explorable 2.5D scenes; "candlelight" relighting of a painting as the pointer moves.

### Milestone 2 takeaways
- **Linking projects with motion:** shared-element morphs (View Transitions; GSAP Flip) are the standard way to make a project card *become* its page. Same-document is Baseline (Oct 2025); cross-document works in Chrome/Safari, Firefox unconfirmed.
- **Painterly rendering tiers:** (1) SVG filters + CSS: widely supported, light; (2) WebGL 2 shaders such as Kuwahara, depth parallax, relighting, craquelure: broad support, moderate effort; (3) WebGPU: an upgrade path only.
- **Scroll-linked effects** can be native CSS in all major browsers as of Firefox 159 (Oct 2026); GSAP ScrollTrigger is the free JS route with broader control.

## Milestone 3: Portfolio UX (RQ5)

**[S29] MIT Admissions: Creative portfolios / Maker Portfolio**
- Publisher: MIT Admissions
- Date: current page; blog FAQs from 2013 and 2021; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://mitadmissions.org/apply/firstyear/portfolios-additional-material/ ; https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/faq-for-maker-portfolios/
- Summary: Optional Maker Portfolio, submitted in SlideRoom and reviewed by MIT faculty and alumni makers. MIT says it is more interested in the build process than the end result and reviews only projects with enough evidence of process. Up to 25 media items, each with a clear explanation, separating finished work from work in progress. Video capped at 2 minutes in total. **Code projects need the codebase plus a working demo, or they cannot be reviewed.** Outside links may not be reviewed.
- Reliability: high (primary).
- **Implication:** colleges may never visit the site. It has to *produce* SlideRoom-ready material (process images, short captions, demo links), and each project page should lead with process.

**[S30] "Scrolling and Attention" (2018 eyetracking update)**
- Publisher: Nielsen Norman Group
- Date: 2018
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/
- Summary: About 57% of page-viewing time is above the fold and 74% in the first two screenfuls (2010: 80% above the fold).
- Reliability: high (primary eyetracking research).
- **Implication:** the first screen must show who you are and a way into the projects. A long animated intro that pushes projects down costs most of the attention.

**[S31] "Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics"**
- Publisher: Nielsen Norman Group
- Date: 2016
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/
- Summary: Quantitative study of 179 participants on six sites. Hiding main navigation cut discoverability "almost in half", made tasks slower and felt harder. The effect was worse on desktop than on mobile.
- Reliability: high for the direction of the effect (primary); exact secondary percentages not verified.
- **Implication:** a painting-as-navigation concept must still keep a visible, plain project index (e.g. a fixed label strip or legend). Exploration should be optional, never the only route.

### Milestone 3 takeaways
- **Three audiences, three needs:**
  - **Colleges:** process evidence, captions, working demos. They may only see SlideRoom, so the site should generate that material.
  - **Recruiters/hiring managers:** fast scan, clear project titles, links to repos and demos.
  - **Clients:** finished results and a contact route.
- **UX guardrails for every concept:**
  1. Name, role and project entry points on the first screen.
  2. A visible, plain project index is always one click away (never hidden behind the art).
  3. Each project page leads with a one-line summary, role, stack and links, then the process story.
  4. Any exploratory or game-like layer is optional.

**[S36] WCAG 2.2 Understanding SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA)**
- Publisher: W3C WAI
- Date: WCAG 2.2 (2023); accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html
- Summary: "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential…" Protects people with vestibular disorders (dizziness, nausea, headaches). Parallax is named as a common non-essential animation. Scroll that moves elements must be controllable. "Motion animation" excludes colour and opacity changes (an erratum says blur should *not* be excluded).
- Reliability: high (primary standard).
- **Implication:** sfumato-style *opacity* reveals are the safe reduced-motion fallback. Parallax, camera moves, panel swings and anamorphic stretching must turn off or simplify.

**[S37] WCAG 2.2 Understanding SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (A)**
- Publisher: W3C WAI
- URL: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html
- Summary: Moving content that starts automatically, lasts more than 5 seconds and runs alongside other content needs a pause/stop/hide control.
- Reliability: high.
- **Implication:** ambient loops (drifting haze, flickering candle, idle 3D camera) need a visible pause control or must stop within 5 seconds.

**[S38] MDN: `prefers-reduced-motion`**
- Publisher: MDN Web Docs
- URL: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion
- Summary: Detects the OS "reduce motion" setting (Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, Linux desktops). "Widely available… since January 2020." The MDN example swaps a scaling animation for an opacity-only dissolve.
- Reliability: high.

**[S39] "How to create high-performance CSS animations" / "Animations and performance"**
- Publisher: web.dev
- URL: https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide ; https://web.dev/articles/animations-and-performance
- Summary: Animate `transform` and `opacity` where possible so work stays on the compositor thread. Animating layout or paint properties loads the main thread. Target 60 fps. Use `will-change` sparingly (each layer costs memory).
- Reliability: high.

**[S40] Core Web Vitals thresholds**
- Publisher: web.dev
- Date: updated 2025-05-07
- URL: https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds
- Summary: "Good" at the 75th percentile: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1. INP replaced FID in March 2024.
- Reliability: high.
- **Implication:** a heavy WebGL intro must not be the Largest Contentful Paint. Render the name/title as HTML first, then load the canvas after it.

**[S41] Three.js on mobile: pixel ratio and render-on-demand**
- Publisher: three.js forum (discourse.threejs.org) threads; three.js manual "Rendering on demand" (not opened)
- Date: various
- URL: https://discourse.threejs.org/t/low-fps-on-ios-mobile-with-pixel-ration-set-as-window-devicepixelratio/4963 ; https://threejs.org/manual/#en/rendering-on-demand
- Summary: Rendering at full device pixel ratio on phones can drop even simple scenes to about 40 fps. Cap it (e.g. `Math.min(devicePixelRatio, 1.5–2)`). Render only when something changes for static scenes. Disable shadows/antialiasing on mobile.
- Reliability: medium (community practice, widely repeated; the manual page was not read).

### Milestone 4 takeaways
**Motion budget for every concept:**
1. HTML-first content (name, project titles, links) so LCP/CLS stay good and the site works without WebGL.
2. Signature motion uses transform/opacity or GPU shaders only.
3. `prefers-reduced-motion` → replace movement with opacity cross-fades (sfumato fallback).
4. Ambient loops stop within 5 s or have a pause control.
5. Cap pixel ratio and render on demand on mobile.
6. WebGL 2 baseline, WebGPU optional.

## Milestone A1: Motion timing (RQ8)

**[S49] "Executing UX Animations: Duration and Motion Characteristics"**
- Publisher: Nielsen Norman Group
- Date: 2020 (approx.); accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/
- Summary:
  - Most UI animations should take 100-500 ms, depending on complexity and travel distance.
  - Simple feedback (toggles) takes about 100 ms and feels immediate. Large screen changes take 200-300 ms. Around 500 ms starts to "feel like a real drag".
  - Use the shortest duration that isn't jarring; animations are far more often too long than too short.
  - The more often an animation is seen, the shorter and subtler it should be.
- Reliability: high (primary UX research organisation).
- **Implication:** frequent animations (hover, panel swap, page change) stay at or under about 400 ms. Only rare, once-per-visit moments (the altarpiece opening) can be longer, and they must be skippable.

**[S50] "Response Times: The 3 Important Limits"**
- Author/Publisher: Jakob Nielsen, NN/g (1993, based on Miller 1968 and Card et al.)
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/
- Summary: 0.1 s feels instantaneous. 1 s keeps the flow of thought; delay is noticed. 10 s is the limit of attention: beyond it you need a progress indicator and a way out.
- Reliability: high (classic, widely cited; values are guidelines).
- **Implications:**
  - Any input must show visible feedback within 0.1 s, even if a longer animation follows.
  - The arrival sequence should finish under about 1-2 s or be skippable.
  - Cold-start demos (MLB 30-60 s, NFL 10-30 s, from P1/P3) exceed 10 s, so the site must warn before linking and offer a recorded preview.

**[S51] Material Components for Android: "Motion" (Material 3 easing and duration tokens)**
- Publisher: Google, material-components-android docs (GitHub)
- Date: current master; accessed 2026-11-13
- URL: https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md
- Summary:
  - Easing curves:
    - standard `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1)`; standard decelerate `(0,0,0,1)`; standard accelerate `(0.3,0,1,1)`
    - emphasized: a path `M0,0 C0.05,0 0.133333,0.06 0.166666,0.4 C0.208333,0.82 0.25,1 1,1`, for common animations that begin and end on screen
    - emphasized decelerate `(0.05,0.7,0.1,1)` for elements entering; emphasized accelerate `(0.3,0,0.8,0.15)` for elements exiting
  - Duration tokens: short1-4 = 50/100/150/200 ms; medium1-4 = 250-400 ms; long1-4 = 450-600 ms; extra-long1-4 = 700-1000 ms.
- Reliability: high (official Google implementation docs). App-oriented, so applying it to an artistic site involves judgment.
- **Implication:** a ready vocabulary of named curves and durations. The two-segment "emphasized" path can be used directly in GSAP CustomEase [S53].

**[S52] GSAP docs: Eases**
- Publisher: GreenSock
- URL: https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Eases/
- Summary: Built-in families include power1-4, expo, sine, circ, back, elastic, bounce and steps, with .in/.out/.inOut. The default ease is "power1.out". Change it with gsap.defaults().
- Reliability: high.

**[S53] GSAP docs: CustomEase**
- Publisher: GreenSock
- URL: https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Eases/CustomEase
- Summary: CustomEase.create(id, data) accepts an SVG path in the 0,0-1,1 space, or a four-value cubic-bezier string. Register it with gsap.registerPlugin(CustomEase).
- Reliability: high (official; details partly via snippets).
- **Implication:** the same named curves can be defined once and shared by CSS (cubic-bezier) and GSAP (CustomEase). One motion language across both.

**Decision (A1):** "Ceremony once". The arrival is about 1.6 s and skippable; repeated motion runs 100-400 ms; reduced motion uses 150 ms opacity (decisions.md #17).

## Milestone A4: Technical architecture (RQ12)

**[S62] Astro docs: View transitions (full page read)**
- Publisher: Astro
- URL: https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/view-transitions/
- Summary:
  - Lifecycle events in order: astro:before-preparation, astro:after-preparation, astro:before-swap, astro:after-swap, astro:page-load. Use **astro:page-load** to re-run setup after every navigation, because bundled module scripts "are only ever executed once". Inline scripts can re-run with `data-astro-rerun`.
  - `transition:persist` keeps an element or island across navigation (CSS animations still restart). `transition:persist-props` also keeps its props.
  - Fallbacks for browsers without the API: `animate` (default, simulated), `swap`, `none`.
  - **`<ClientRouter />` disables all view-transition animations, including fallbacks, under prefers-reduced-motion.**
- Reliability: high (official docs, read directly). Supersedes the fragmentary S46 reading.
- **Implication:** reduced motion for page transitions comes free. The site must still supply its own 150 ms opacity cross-fade if a gentle fade is wanted (the decision #17 spec).

**[S63] GSAP docs: gsap.matchMedia()**
- Publisher: GreenSock
- URL: https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/
- Summary: Runs setup only while a media query matches, e.g. `(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` or `(min-width: 800px)`. Animations and ScrollTriggers made inside are reverted automatically when the query stops matching. It wraps gsap.context(). An optional cleanup function can be returned.
- Reliability: high.

**[S64] GSAP docs: Flip plugin**
- Publisher: GreenSock
- URL: https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/Flip/
- Summary: Flip.getState() records the layout, you change the DOM, then Flip.from(state, {duration, ease, absolute, scale, nested}) animates between them. getState() completes any running flip first, so interruptions are handled. `scale: true` usually performs better than animating width and height. Flip.batch() is for coordinated flips; Flip.killFlipsOf() and Flip.isFlipping() also exist.
- Reliability: high.

**[S65] Astro docs: Images**
- Publisher: Astro
- URL: https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/images/
- Summary: `<Image />` and `<Picture />` from astro:assets optimise images in `src/` (size, format, quality) at build time. The rendered img "infers image dimensions to avoid Cumulative Layout Shift". `<Picture />` outputs AVIF/WebP plus a fallback. A `layout` property generates srcset/sizes. `alt` is required. Files in `public/` are copied unprocessed.
- Reliability: high.

**[S66] "The Performance Inequality Gap, 2024"**
- Author: Alex Russell (Infrequently Noted)
- Date: January 2024
- URL: https://infrequently.org/2024/01/performance-inequality-gap-2024/
- Summary: P75 first-load budgets.
  - Markup-centric sites: 3 s target is about 1.4 MiB total compressed critical path, including **75 KiB JS**; 5 s target is about 2.5 MiB, including 100 KiB JS.
  - JS-heavy sites: 3 s target is about 730 KiB, including 365 KiB JS.
  - Baseline: a 2020-21 mid-range Android (Galaxy A51 / Pixel 4a class) on 7.2 Mbps down / 94 ms RTT.
- Reliability: medium-high (an influential practitioner's analysis with stated method; not a standard).
- **Implication:** treat the portfolio as markup-centric. Keep **≤ 75 KiB JS and ≤ 1.4 MiB total on the critical path** of the home page. GSAP, the lens and WebGL load after LCP (idle/visible), outside the critical path.

**[P4-reuse] User's own Falling Constellations repo**
- Source: https://github.com/arinb44/Falling-Constellations (HANDOFF.md)
- Summary: It already has a phone base scale and adaptive-quality controller (quality.js), WebGL context-loss handling, a visibility pause, a `?nogl` 2D fallback and a `?debug` frame-time readout, all unit-tested.
- **Implication:** the portfolio's WebGL layer can reuse the user's own tested quality controller and fallback approach instead of starting from scratch.

## Follow-up after A6: closing the spec's open questions (2026-11-13)

**[S76] MDN: Window.localStorage**
- Publisher: MDN Web Docs
- URL: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/localStorage
- Summary: localStorage data "is saved across browser sessions" and "has no expiration time", while sessionStorage data is cleared when the page session ends (the page is closed). In private/incognito windows, localStorage is cleared when the last private tab closes.
- Reliability: high (primary docs; key sentences read directly).
- Design use: the arrival stores one `arrivedAt` timestamp and plays again only after 30 days (decision #23). It holds no personal data.

**[S77] Own measurement: NBA Predictor live demo load time**
- Source: https://machinelearningnba-p3cxa6nsk6hgcuclcwssdx.streamlit.app (link from the MachineLearningNBA README), loaded in the built-in browser on 2026-11-13.
- Method: timed with `performance.now()` from navigation start until the app's "Daily slate" content was shown and Streamlit's run indicator ("Stop") had cleared.
- Results:
  - **Cold load:** the page was still blank at ~6.5 s; the sidebar appeared by ~58.6 s; the content was ready at ~67 s at the latest (somewhere between 59 and 67 s). Streamlit started the app on its own, with no "wake up" button.
  - **Warm reload right after:** content ready at ~2.0 s.
- Reliability: medium. This is one sample of each from one connection, not a benchmark. The cold time will vary with Streamlit Community Cloud load.
- **Implication:** NBA behaves like MLB (30–60 s, P3). It needs `coldStartSeconds` set (~60), a cold-start note, and a recorded preview. Three ~10 s previews keep the video total at ~115 s, within MIT's 2-minute limit [S29] (decision #24).
